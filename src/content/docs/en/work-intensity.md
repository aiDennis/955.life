The more hours you work, the thinner your life gets. That's the problem 955 is trying to fix.

## The data: China vs. the world

- China: ~**2,195 hours** per year (various estimates); the National Bureau of Statistics reported a weekly average of about **49 hours** in 2023-2024, above the 44-hour legal ceiling.
- Compare: Mexico ~2,207h, South Korea ~1,896h, Japan ~1,607h, US ~1,705h, UK ~1,524h, Germany ~**1,343h**.
- Germany works the least and still leads in productivity. Intensity and efficiency are not the same thing.

## Overwork research

- A WHO/ILO study (2016 data, published 2021) estimated ~**745,000 deaths per year** globally from working 55+ hours weekly.
- Japan enshrined "karoshi" in law (2014 Anti-Karoshi Act). China is beginning to debate overwork protections.

## A timeline of cases (2016-2026)

- **2016**: 58.com exposed for company-wide 996 with unpaid weekend work.
- **2019**: Youzan announces 996 at annual meeting; JD.com reportedly 995/996. 996.ICU explodes — 270k+ GitHub stars.
- **2021**: An e-commerce worker dies on the way home after a late shift — nationwide reckoning over "big/small weeks."
- **2023**: CETC Chengdu overtime incident trends; "forced overtime pushback" resonates.
- **2025-2026**: The wind is shifting. Sichuan Jingyuan adopts a 4-day week, Jilin suggests 4.5 days, provinces encourage 2.5-day weekends, Fujian firms codify the right to disconnect.

The "workaholism is honorable" culture is loosening. Rest isn't laziness. It's part of the job, and cutting back on overtime buys back sleep, family time and hobbies. That's not a loss.
