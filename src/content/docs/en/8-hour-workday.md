The 8-hour workday is the legal foundation of a 955 life, and it took workers more than a century to win it. These days the "hustle" narrative keeps trying to talk people out of it.

## A standard won by history

- **May 1, 1886**: Chicago workers strike for "8 hours work, 8 hours rest, 8 hours for ourselves" — the origin of International Workers' Day.
- **1914**: Ford Motor Company adopts the 8-hour day with $5 daily pay; productivity and profit rise.
- **1919**: The ILO Hours of Work Convention sets 8 hours/day, 48 hours/week.
- **1938**: The US Fair Labor Standards Act establishes the 40-hour week and overtime pay.

## Legal basis in China

- **Constitution, Article 43**: Workers have the right to rest.
- **Labor Law, Article 36**: Daily work **no more than 8 hours**, weekly average **no more than 44**.
- **Labor Law, Article 41**: Overtime generally max 1 hour/day, up to 3 in special cases, **max 36 hours/month**.
- **State Council Regulations**: **8 hours per day, 40 hours per week**.

In other words, **996 (72 hours/week) violates the law by any standard**.

## Global status

As our comparison chart shows: Germany averages ~1,343 hours/year, Japan ~1,607, China ~2,195. Interestingly, the short-hours countries are not less productive. Working less and producing more turn out to be compatible.

## The AI era: the problem is not solved

AI has lifted productivity dramatically, yet average working hours have not come down — the so-called "productivity paradox." The time saved by technology is being reinvested into more output instead of earlier finishing times. In South Korea, a Bank of Korea study found AI cut workers' hours by just 3.8% (about 1.5 hours a week) while overall productivity barely moved. Think tanks estimate that if the gains were shared, 28% of the UK workforce and 35 million US workers could move to a four-day week within a decade. At bottom, working hours are an institutional choice, not a technological inevitability. Iceland, the UK, and the 2026 Sichuan four-day-week pilots all prove the point.
